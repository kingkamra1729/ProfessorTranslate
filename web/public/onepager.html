Live lecture translation that refuses to translate the vocabulary.

professortranslate.onrender.com github.com/kingkamra1729/ProfessorTranslate

The professor says: “So the eigenvalue of this matrix tells us how much the eigenvector is stretched.”

तो इस matrix का eigenvalue बताता है कि eigenvector कितना stretch होता है।

One sentence, two voices, in the student’s earpiece. The explanation is translated. The vocabulary never is.

Who this is for

A first-year engineering student who thinks in Hindi or Bengali

They passed the entrance exam. They can read the textbook, slowly. But the lecture is delivered in English at speaking pace, and by the time they have decoded one sentence the professor is three sentences further on.

They are not failing because the material is hard. They are failing because it arrives in a language they are still translating in their head. This is not a hypothetical user — it is most of a classroom, in most colleges in India.

The barrier

Not the terminology. The explanation.

A student can look up eigenvalue. What they cannot look up is the sentence the lecturer improvised to make it land — “so it tells you how much the vector gets stretched” — the aside that connected it to last week, the “think about why that must be true.” That sentence is never written down. It exists for four seconds and is gone.

And the obvious fix makes it worse:

तो इस आव्यूह का अभिलक्षणिक मान… What an ordinary translator produces. Correct, literary, and a word that appears in no textbook, no exam, and no job interview.

The student now understands the sentence and cannot answer the question. The sentence got easier; the degree got harder.

How it works

Four decisions, each one measurable

The model never sees the terms

Each is replaced by a sentinel before translation and spliced back after. It is not asked nicely to preserve them — it is never given them. Measured at 0% loss across 162 translations.

The same pass decides the voice

Restoring a term is the only moment we know which characters are vocabulary and which are explanation. That boundary drives both the highlight and the voice switch, so the subtitle and the audio always agree.

It speaks the way people speak

Real classroom Hindi code-mixes: अब देखते हैं कि determinant zero हो तो क्या होता है. A translation that sends a student to a dictionary has failed at its one job.

It catches its own gaps

The glossary is the single point of failure, so the system listens for vocabulary it does not yet protect and offers it to the professor mid-lecture. Accepting one protects it for the rest of the class.

Beyond translation

Built for the student who also cannot see the board

Every diagram carries a spoken description, translated by the same term-preserving path — so a plot is heard, not missed. Diagrams are rendered by Wolfram but published only once the professor approves them, because an unreviewed generated diagram on two hundred screens teaches the wrong thing very efficiently.

Recordings are archived as text, not audio, so a lecture can be re-voiced afterwards in a language nobody chose that day, at any speed, or read by a screen reader.

What is real

Everything above runs. Nothing is mocked.

The one thing we did not build is speech. Recognition and synthesis run in the browser — free, keyless — because a lecturer should be able to walk into a hall and use this without anyone having provisioned anything.

A device with no Hindi voice installed produces silence. The app detects that and says so rather than failing quietly: open /check on any device to see. Phones almost always have the voices; Windows laptops often do not.
